The call

Geofence the resort, sell to people standing in it, and stop pre-building by hotel alone.

CLAUDE.md rejected Meta ads because 300 to 500 NOK per destination never exits the learning phase. That objection holds for cold national targeting and it does not hold here. An audience of tourists physically inside Ayia Napa is small, cheap to reach, and it is the only place on earth where the ad is literally true. The quiz is what makes the click worth paying for: the visitor answers four questions and sees their own twelve before they are asked for anything.

What the pivot changes
  • Personalisation axis. Hotel zone only becomes hotel zone plus stated taste.
  • Pages built. Six becomes thirty six, six zones by six taste profiles, still static.
  • Quality bar. Twelve places had to be right. Now all fifty one do.
  • Channel. A conversation on a sunbed becomes a geofenced Meta campaign.
  • Price. EUR 29 was held to protect ten in person sales that can no longer happen.
What survives untouched
  • No backend, no dependencies. The quiz scores client side, the pages are pre-built.
  • Instant delivery. Stripe redirects straight to the page. No email, no wait.
  • Buyer pages stay noindex. Thirty six URLs instead of six, same headers.
  • No Tripadvisor, anywhere. The check already fails the build on the string.
  • Twelve shown, not fifty one. The cut is the product. The quiz only changes which twelve.

The hidden cost, and it is the expensive part

Personalisation promotes the shortlist to the front page. Only 14 of 51 places carry both a bus number and a price today, which is harmless while 37 of them sit in a tier nobody sees. Under the quiz any of them can be the first entry a buyer reads. Three of those gaps are already in the verdict twelve and pass the check only because their mode is walk. Thirty eight missing fields move from optional to blocking, and only Vegard can close them. Every engineering task in Phase 1 ships into a product that stays wrong until that research lands.

What makes destination two cheap

Three hardcoded Ayia Napa strings sit in scripts/build.mjs and both data files are flat at the repo root. Move them to a folder per destination and the build stops caring which resort it is rendering. After that, adding Rhodes costs research time and zero engineering time, which is the only version of scalable that matters here. The quiz taxonomy carries over unchanged, and it already covers a food list as well as an activity list: the /jk page proves the same eight categories work for restaurants.

What breaks this

Four ways the plan fails, ranked by how likely they are.

Most likely

Nobody pays, and the ads make that expensive to learn

Face to face, a rejection cost a conversation. Paid, it costs the click. The break even sits near five percent of quiz finishers at EUR 29 and EUR 0.50 a click. Write the kill rule before the spend starts: stop at 300 clicks with zero sales, and treat that as an answer rather than a setback.

Near certain

The research debt gates everything downstream

Thirty seven bus numbers and one price, and no amount of code closes them. Build the quiz anyway, because the check will refuse to ship the campaign until the fields are filled, which turns a list of things to remember into a build that fails loudly.

Structural

A geofence over one resort saturates in days

Ayia Napa holds tens of thousands of tourists at peak, not millions. Frequency climbs fast, the creative burns out, and the season ends regardless. Treat the campaign as a short sharp test of willingness to pay, not as a channel that runs all year.

Accepted for now

Two buyers with the same hotel and the same taste share a URL

Under face to face selling that leak was small. Under ads it scales with the campaign. C6 in CLAUDE.md, a signed per buyer slug issued by a Netlify Function, was parked until the first ten sales. That trigger is unchanged. It stays parked.
